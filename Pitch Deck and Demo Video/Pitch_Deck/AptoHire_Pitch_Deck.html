The story

A recruiter rejected the same CV twice.

On the third application he got an interview, an instant offer, and a year later he was a Team Leader performing exceptionally well.

An HR professional's LinkedIn post: 22K likes · 626 comments · 913 reposts. A signal of how widely this is felt.

That was luck. A system should not depend on a recruiter giving someone a third chance.

Raju's resume isn't shortlisted. Riya has no proof yet. Priya has the skill, not the profile. AptoHire replaces luck with proof.

The solution

Proof of work, not claims of work.

1

Build

An AI recommender gives a real project at the student's level; the student records a 3-minute demo.

2

Verify

AI agents audit video, audio, script and code against a rubric; every score links to evidence. Humans check borderline cases.

3

Get found

An AI matching agent ranks anonymous candidates with reasons. Identity is revealed only if the student says yes.

AI proposes. Rules decide. Identity, consent and permissions never depend on AI.

Fusion + rubric

Clear and sufficient: scorecard with score, rationale, evidence references and confidence. Borderline or conflicting: human review or appeal.

Trust safeguards

Schema-checked outputs · rubric version stored · bounded retries · kill switch · we measure false passes, false fails and AI-human agreement (target 80%+).

Pass mark (50) and the 40-60 review band are proposed thresholds to calibrate in the pilot. Accent, camera quality and AI-use disclosure are not penalised.

Scalability strategy

Land. Expand. Become the standard.

Land

2 pilot universities, one IT/CS department, 50-100 students, 3-5 employers.

Expand

Department → campus → state clusters through TPO networks; employers follow verified talent.

Standard

ATS and LMS integrations, AI skill interviews, Hindi and regional languages.

Tech: AI agents are modular: a new skill family needs a new rubric and templates, not new code. One database, row-level security.

Cost curve: AI cost per review ₹12 → ₹4.5; human-check share 15% → 8% (assumptions to measure).

Moat grows with use: validated scores, college relationships, consent trust.
